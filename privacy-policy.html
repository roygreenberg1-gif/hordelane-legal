Privacy Policy for Hordelane

Last updated: 7 October 2026

Hordelane: Zombie Survival ("Hordelane", "the game") is developed and published by Roy Greenberg. This policy explains what information the game collects, why, and how to switch it off. It applies to the Android application with the package name com.greenberg.hordelane, and to the iPhone application with the bundle identifier com.greenberg.hordelane on the App Store. The two are the same game, but the iPhone version leaves some parts out: On iPhone below says exactly where they differ. Everything else on this page applies to both.

The short version

The game collects gameplay statistics — which level you played, whether you won, and what you upgraded — so that the difficulty and pacing can be improved. None of it carries your name, email, contacts, photos, precise location, or anything else that identifies you personally. You choose, the first time you play and again at any time in Settings, whether those statistics may carry a random tag that lets us find and delete yours (statistics sent that way are pseudonymous: tied to random identifiers, never to your name or account), or whether they are sent as anonymous statistics only, with no identifier at all.

Rewarded videos are the one kind of advertising in the game, and they play only when you ask for one: WATCH AD to get back into a run you have just lost or to refill a heart, or CHANGE MISSION on the missions board to swap a mission for another. They are served by Google's ad service, AdMob, which reads your device's advertising ID and a few other things described under Rewarded videos below, and uses them for its own purposes under Google's own privacy policy. Nothing you do in the game is sold to anyone.

Backing your progress up to Google Play Games is a separate, optional sign-in. It is the only part of the game that involves an account, and the only part that sees a name — the gamer name on your own Google Play Games profile. The game is fully playable without it.

On iPhone

The iPhone version differs from the Android one in these ways, and only these:

  • Saying no means nothing is sent. The question's two answers on iPhone are YES, CONTINUE and NO, THANKS. With YES, the statistics are sent exactly as described below for that answer. With NO, the analytics component is never started: no gameplay statistics, no app instance identifier, no DATA ID, no device information and no app-open events are sent at all. Nothing is sent before you answer either, because on iPhone the analytics component does not start until you say yes. In Settings the two modes read GAME DATA FULL and GAME DATA OFF, and CHANGE switches between them; switching to OFF stops the analytics at once, and it stays off when the game is opened again.
  • No advertising. The iPhone version contains no ad service and shows no advertisements, so the Rewarded videos section does not apply to it. The game does not track you across other companies' apps or websites, never asks for App Tracking Transparency permission, and its analytics is built without the component that could read the advertising identifier.
  • No account and no cloud backup. There is no Google Play Games sign-in on iPhone, so Backing up your progress does not apply: your progress is stored on the iPhone. If you use your iPhone's own backup (iCloud Backup, or a backup to a computer), that backup may include the game's saved data, as it does for most apps; that copy is in your Apple account or on your computer, and the developer cannot read it.
  • Purchases go through Apple. Payments are handled entirely by the App Store, under Apple's terms; the developer never receives, sees, or stores your card number, billing address, or any other payment detail. Survivors, weapons and other items you own permanently can be brought back on a new install or another iPhone with RESTORE PURCHASES in Settings; HORDE PASS PREMIUM, a purchase for one season that does not renew, comes back the same way for the season it was bought in. A purchase that waits for a parent's approval (Ask to Buy) is delivered once Apple completes it; while it waits, the game only remembers, until it is closed, that it is waiting. Refunds are handled by Apple, at reportaproblem.apple.com. The purchase component the game uses (Unity's In-App Purchasing) talks only to the App Store on iPhone: the iPhone version is built with its purchase report to Unity switched off, so Unity receives no purchase record and no device information from it.
  • Nothing to the game engine's maker. The iPhone version is built with Unity's own analytics, performance reporting and crash reporting switched off, so the app sends nothing to Unity Technologies.
  • The time, from Apple. To keep timed rewards fair, the iPhone version asks Apple's web server (www.apple.com) for the current time each time the game starts, the way a browser loads a page. The request carries nothing about you or your play, and the answer is used only on the iPhone. Apple receives your IP address, as with any web request, under Apple's own privacy policy. This happens whichever way you answered the statistics question, because it is not a statistic: nothing about you is collected by it.
  • Age range, where the law requires it. Some places require apps to check a user's age through the app store; Texas does for new accounts. Where Apple reports that such a law applies, the game asks Apple, through Apple's age-range feature, which age range the account belongs to. Apple decides whether to ask you or a parent and what it shares. The answer is used only on the iPhone, only while the game is running, and only to apply age rules. It is never stored, never sent to the developer, and never used for anything else. Purchases by minors go through Apple's own parental approval. If Apple reports that a player is under 13, the game stops sending statistics for that player as soon as Apple's answer arrives, whatever was answered on the consent card, and asks Google Analytics to discard anything not yet uploaded. Events from the first moments of that session may already have been sent; email us with the DATA ID and they will be deleted.
  • No permissions. The iPhone version asks for no permissions: no tracking, location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos, or notifications.
  • Device information sent with the statistics, when you have said yes, names the iPhone model and the iOS version where the table below says Android version.

What the game does not collect

  • No email address and no phone number. No name and no account either, unless you choose to sign in to Google Play Games to back your progress up — that sign-in is optional and is described under Backing up your progress below.
  • No advertising identifier in the statistics. Google's ad service reads your advertising ID for the rewarded videos; the game's own analytics is set not to read it, so none of the records described above carry it.
  • No precise or GPS location.
  • No contacts, photos, files, microphone, or camera access.
  • No text you type. The game has no chat and no user-generated content.
  • The game requests no Android runtime permissions, and asks for no permissions on iPhone.

Backing up your progress (Android)

The game can back your progress up to Google Play Games so that a new phone, or a reinstall, does not cost you your campaign. This is optional. If you decline, the game plays exactly as before and your progress stays on the device and nowhere else.

If you do sign in, two things happen. Your saved progress — the stage you have reached, your coins, and which survivors and weapons you own — is stored in your own Google account. And Google tells the game your gamer name and avatar from your Play Games profile. That is the only name the game ever sees, and it is a name you chose for Play Games rather than your legal one.

This saved data lives in your Google account, not on any server belonging to the developer. The developer cannot read it, and cannot delete it for you. You remove it the same way you would for any other game: through the Play Games app, or through your Google account settings.

Signing in and the GAME DATA switch in Settings are two separate choices, and neither one turns the other on. Declining the statistics does not sign you out, and signing in does not start any collection you had switched off.

The app instance identifier

The identifier is generated randomly when you install the game. It is not your device's serial number, not your advertising ID, and is not linked to any account. On its own it cannot identify you, and it changes if you uninstall and reinstall the game or clear its data.

On iPhone, Google Analytics also records Apple's identifier for vendors (IDFV): a random ID that Apple gives to one developer's apps on one device. It is not the advertising identifier, it is not linked to any account, and it is reset when all of this developer's apps are removed from the device.

You are asked how much to share, before anything can be traced to you

The first time you reach the safe house, the game asks how it may measure your play, and lists what that covers. Nothing that can be traced to you is sent before you answer. Until the question is settled, Google Analytics is told that storage is denied, so the few events the Firebase component sends on its own when the app starts — that it was opened, and that a session began — carry no app instance identifier and no DATA ID, and cannot be linked to you or to one another. The gameplay statistics described above are held back entirely until you have answered. The question cannot be closed without answering it. There are two answers on Android (on iPhone, see On iPhone above):

  • YES, CONTINUE. The statistics above are sent together with the app instance identifier and your DATA ID, so that your play can be counted as one player's over time and so that a deletion request from you can be carried out.
  • ANONYMOUS STATS ONLY. The same gameplay statistics are sent, but Google Analytics is told that storage is denied: no app instance identifier is created or kept, the DATA ID is not attached, and the events cannot be linked to you or to one another. Google still receives, with each event, the device model, the Android version, the app version, the time, and a country derived from the IP address, which is not stored. Because nothing identifies you in this mode, there is no record of yours to delete.

Players who installed the game before this question existed were not shown it. Their play is measured in the first way, which is what the earlier version of this policy set out, and they were told so directly, in the channel the closed test uses. They can switch to anonymous statistics only in Settings at any time.

How to change your mind, in either direction

Open Settings inside the game: it shows GAME DATA FULL or ANONYMOUS ONLY, and the CHANGE button under it switches to the other (in earlier versions of the game, tap the mode itself); each tap switches again. The change takes effect immediately: from anonymous only, nothing further carries an identifier; from full, the tag is attached again from that moment. The switch is always there, whichever way you answered the first question, and your choice is remembered. The game is identical in both modes — no feature, reward, or level is withheld.

Purchases

On Android, payments are handled entirely by Google Play (on iPhone, by the App Store - see On iPhone above). The developer never receives, sees, or stores your card number, billing address, or any other payment detail. When a purchase succeeds, Google Play hands the game a signed receipt naming the product and an order number; the game checks the signature and then delivers the item.

The game records that a purchase happened — which product, and so its price tier, and when — and, when a purchase does not go through, which product it was, the level, and the reason it did not (for example: cancelled, no connection, payment declined, or payment still pending), as analytics events, so that pricing and pacing can be judged and failing purchases can be found. These records carry the same random DATA ID as everything else and contain no payment information, no order number, and no name.

Refunds are handled by Google Play on Android and by Apple on iPhone. The Terms of Service explain what a purchase gives you.

Rewarded videos (Android only)

The game shows one kind of advertisement: a rewarded video, which plays only when you ask for one: WATCH AD to get back into a run you have just lost or to refill a heart, or CHANGE MISSION on the missions board to swap a mission for another. Nothing plays on its own, between levels or in the lobby, and a video you close before the end simply pays nothing. If you never tap the button, the ad service is still started when the game starts and fetches a video in advance, so that the button works when you do.

The videos are served by Google AdMob, part of Google LLC. When the game asks Google for a video, and while it plays, Google's software collects — in Google's own words — your device's IP address, which it may use to estimate a general location; your interactions with the video, such as that it was requested, played or tapped; diagnostic information about how the app performed while it ran; and device identifiers, the Android advertising ID and the app set ID. Google uses that data for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and it may use the advertising ID to choose which video to show you.

This is the one place where data leaves the game for a company's own use rather than for processing on the developer's behalf, which is why the Data Safety section on Google Play lists it as shared. Google handles it under Google's privacy policy and explains its advertising in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services. The developer receives none of it: the game learns only whether the video finished, and records that as one of the gameplay events described above, without the advertising ID.

Your controls. Android lets you reset or delete the advertising ID and switch off ads personalisation — Settings → Google → Ads on most devices. With the ID deleted, Google receives a string of zeros in its place and the videos still play. You can also manage ad personalisation for your Google account at adssettings.google.com. The button decides whether a video is ever shown to you — none is shown unasked — but not whether one is fetched in advance, as described above; these settings are the control for that.

Who processes the data

Analytics are processed by Google Firebase Analytics on the developer's behalf. Google acts as a data processor and is bound by the Firebase Data Processing Terms. See also Firebase privacy information.

If you sign in to Google Play Games, that part is not processed on the developer’s behalf at all: the saved progress and your gamer profile sit inside your own Google account, under Google’s privacy policy and the terms you already have with Google. The developer receives only what the game needs to restore a save.

Purchases go through Unity's In-App Purchasing service, the component the game uses to talk to Google Play (on iPhone it is built without this report, and Unity receives nothing - see On iPhone above). When you buy something, Unity Technologies receives the purchase record — the product, the Google Play receipt and its signature, the order number, the device platform, and a session identifier for the game's Unity project — and uses it to confirm the purchase and to report purchase statistics to the developer. That processing is described in Unity's privacy policy for players of Unity-made games. Unity does not receive your card number or billing address either; those never leave Google Play.

Rewarded videos are served by Google AdMob, which is not a processor acting on the developer's behalf: Google decides for itself what it does with the data its advertising software collects, as described under Rewarded videos, and is bound to you by Google's privacy policy.

The gameplay statistics are not sold, rented, or shared with advertisers, data brokers, or any other third party; they are used only to improve this game. The only data that reaches an advertising company is what Google's own ad service collects when the game fetches and plays rewarded videos, described above.

Where your progress is stored

Your saved game — level reached, coins, upgrades, settings, and reward timers — is stored locally on your device. It is not readable by the developer, and uninstalling the game removes that local copy.

If you sign in to Google Play Games, a copy is also kept in your own Google account so that it survives a reinstall — see Backing up your progress above. Without that sign-in, nothing about your progress leaves the device.

How long it is kept

In the Firebase Analytics reporting console, event data is retained for 14 months and is then deleted automatically.

A copy of the raw event records is also exported to Google BigQuery, where it is kept only for as long as it is useful for improving the game, so that changes can be compared over time. These records contain only the gameplay information described above and the random identifiers described above — no name, account, contact details, or advertising identifier. Records are deleted on request, and in any case when they are no longer useful for improving the game. What Google's ad service collects for the rewarded videos is held by Google under its own retention rules, not in these records.

Your rights, and how to actually use them

Depending on where you live you may have the right to access, correct, or delete data relating to you, and to object to its processing.

Data tied only to random identifiers creates a practical problem for those rights: without something to match on, no one can tell which records are yours. The game therefore shows you a DATA ID — a random 12-character tag created on your device when you first play. Open Settings and it is printed on the line under the title; tap that line to copy it.

To have your data deleted, email the address below and include your DATA ID. Records carrying that tag will be deleted. Please send the request before uninstalling: the tag is stored only on your device — it is deliberately left out of the Play Games backup — and once the game is removed it cannot be recovered by you or by the developer.

To stop being identified without deleting anything, switch to ANONYMOUS ONLY in Settings (GAME DATA OFF on iPhone, which stops sending anything at all); from then on nothing sent carries your DATA ID or any other identifier. Records sent before the switch keep the tag and can still be deleted by the request above, as long as you copy the DATA ID first.

The DATA ID covers the statistics, and only those. If you signed in to Google Play Games, your backed-up progress sits in your own Google account rather than in the developer’s records, so it is removed through the Play Games app or your Google account settings — not by the email request above.

If you are in California

Under the California Consumer Privacy Act, the categories of personal information collected are identifiers (the random app instance identifier, the DATA ID and, on iPhone, Apple's identifier for vendors), internet or other electronic network activity (the gameplay events listed above), and commercial information (which in-app products were bought, without any payment detail). They are collected directly from the app on your device, for the business purpose of analysing and improving the game. When the game fetches or plays a rewarded video, Google's ad service also collects the advertising ID and IP address described under Rewarded videos, for its own purposes.

Your personal information is not sold. Since 17 September 2026 one thing may count as "sharing" under California's definition: when the game fetches a rewarded video, Google's ad service receives your advertising ID and IP address and may use them to choose ads, which is cross-context behavioural advertising in the statute's terms. It happens whenever the game fetches a video, which it does in advance from the moment it starts, not only when you tap WATCH AD. To opt out, delete the advertising ID and switch off ads personalisation in Android's settings and at adssettings.google.com, as described under Rewarded videos; Google then receives a string of zeros in place of the ID. The developer holds no copy of the identifier and does not sell or share anything else.

You have the right to know what is collected, to request deletion, to request correction, and not to be discriminated against for exercising those rights. The game is identical whether or not you opt out. To make a request, follow the DATA ID instructions above.

If you are in another US state

A number of US states now have their own consumer privacy laws. The details differ, but the rights they give overlap heavily, and this game treats every US resident the same way rather than working out which state you are in.

You may ask what is collected about you, ask for it to be deleted, ask for it to be corrected, and object to its processing. The route is the one described above: send your DATA ID to the address at the bottom of this page.

Your personal information is not sold. The one case of sharing for targeted advertising is the rewarded video, described under Rewarded videos and in the California section above, and the way out of it is the same: delete the advertising ID and switch off ads personalisation in Android's settings. The game does not profile you for decisions that have legal or similarly significant effects, and it collects none of the categories those laws treat as sensitive: no precise location, no health data, no biometrics, and nothing about race, religion, or immigration status.

The game behaves identically whether or not you exercise any of these rights.

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom

The developer is the data controller. The legal basis for processing gameplay analytics that carry an identifier is consent, which you may withdraw at any time by switching to ANONYMOUS ONLY in Settings (GAME DATA OFF on iPhone); withdrawing does not affect processing that already took place. Anonymous statistics carry no identifier and are processed on the basis of the developer's legitimate interest in balancing and improving the game. You may also lodge a complaint with your national supervisory authority. Data is processed by Google on servers that may be located outside the EEA, under Google's standard contractual clauses. The game, on Android and on iPhone, is not distributed in the EEA or the UK, and the rewarded videos are requested without a consent dialog for that reason; before any release there, a consent request meeting Google's requirements would be shown before the first video is fetched.

Children

Hordelane is not directed at children under 13. It is not listed in Google Play's Families programme or in the App Store's Kids category. The game does not knowingly collect information from children: on iPhone, where Apple's age-range feature reports a player under 13, the game stops sending statistics for that player as soon as that report arrives, and data already sent is deleted on request with the DATA ID.
